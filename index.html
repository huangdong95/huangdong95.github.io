My research interests include household finance, behavioral finance, and FinTech & AI.

I study how institutional arrangements and information frictions shape individuals' financial decisions, using causal inference, machine learning, large language models, and Bayesian econometrics.

My job market paper explores an implication of consumer behavior for the boundaries of the firm: delegating unpopular actions to an external agent can protect a firm's customer relationships from damage caused by the agent's conduct—a blame-shifting benefit of outsourcing that firms should consider.

I hold a Master of Finance, a B.Econ. in Economics & Finance, and a B.Sc. in Pure & Applied Mathematics from Tsinghua University.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

References: James J. Choi (Chair), William N. Goetzmann, Paul Goldsmith-Pinkham, and Nicholas C. Barberis
